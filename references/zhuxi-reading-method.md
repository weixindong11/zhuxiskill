# Zhu Xi Reading Method For Scholarly Literature

This reference distills the source folder's repeated themes into a modern workflow for important literature, especially theoretical writing.

## Source Principles

### 纲领

Treat reading as a way to grasp a text's governing principle, not as note accumulation. For modern theory, the governing principle is usually the problem, conceptual distinction, mechanism, or explanatory model that orders the paper.

Ask:

- What is this text trying to make intelligible?
- What does the author think existing readers misunderstand?
- What is the smallest claim that, if removed, makes the whole text collapse?

### 循序渐进

Read in order at two levels:

- `群书之序`: choose prerequisite or easier texts before harder ones when the user is building a field map.
- `一书之序`: follow the current text's chapter, section, paragraph, and concept order before jumping to debate or critique.

For one paper, make a reading ladder:

1. bibliographic identity and context,
2. research problem,
3. central concepts,
4. main argument,
5. evidence or examples,
6. implications,
7. later disputes or extensions.

### 前人研究的批判、承接与拓展

Treat literature review passages as part of the argument. The author is usually creating theoretical room for the current contribution.

Track these moves:

- `承接`: what the author accepts as useful.
- `批判`: what limitation, blind spot, ambiguity, or wrong emphasis the author identifies.
- `补足`: what missing mechanism, process, dimension, context, value, or boundary condition the author adds.
- `转向`: how the author changes the problem frame.
- `整合`: which strands are combined into a larger architecture.
- `重定位`: whether prior work becomes an antecedent, special case, contrast class, measurement precedent, or partial explanation.
- `操作化`: how prior ideas are converted into measures, tasks, scenarios, coding categories, or research designs.

Use this table:

```markdown
| Prior work/tradition | What it contributes | Limitation named by author | Author's move | Resulting theoretical space | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  | 承接/批判/补足/转向/整合/重定位/操作化 |  |  |
```

In Sternberg's balance theory, earlier explicit theories such as Baltes' wisdom-as-expert-knowledge program are not merely summarized. They provide a measurement and life-problem precedent, while Sternberg shifts the theory toward balancing processes and the goal of common good. His own 1990 theory is repositioned as specifying antecedents, whereas the 1998 balance theory specifies processes and goal.

### 熟读精思

Understanding comes from rereading short units. Repeatedly paraphrase the same paragraph at increasing precision:

- `初译`: ordinary-language meaning.
- `细读`: what every clause contributes.
- `贯通`: how this passage links backward and forward.
- `反问`: what it rules out, assumes, or leaves unresolved.

For English theory texts, keep key terms bilingual. Translate naturally, but preserve technical terms in parentheses on first use.

### 概念澄清与辨析

Important theory texts often develop by clarifying terms, not only by adding claims. Treat these passages as central.

Track:

- `definition`: the author's explicit definition.
- `clarification`: a sentence that prevents likely misunderstanding.
- `comparison`: a contrast with a neighboring concept or prior theory.
- `extension`: where one term is said to include and go beyond another.
- `boundary`: examples of what belongs, what does not, and why.
- `term choice`: why the author chooses one word rather than another.

Use a concept-difference table:

```markdown
| Concept A | Concept B | Author's distinction | Why it matters | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |
```

When an author says one concept includes but goes beyond another, do not collapse them. In Sternberg's balance theory, `interests` includes `points of view`, but goes beyond it by adding motivational and affective divergences, not just cognitive perspectives. This distinction matters because wisdom must understand what people want and feel, not only what they think.

### 虚心涵泳

Suspend the urge to make the text fit an existing opinion. First let the author's words set the frame.

Use these checks:

- Am I using a later theory to explain the author before the author has been heard?
- Am I turning an ambiguous sentence into my preferred claim?
- Have I distinguished textual evidence from my inference?
- Have I allowed the text's quiet or plain-looking sentence to carry weight?

### 切己体察

Bring the text back to the reader's own work only after internal reconstruction.

For research:

- What variables, mechanisms, constructs, or assumptions does this sharpen?
- What empirical case would reveal the theory's strength or weakness?
- What does the theory make newly visible?

For teaching:

- What classroom example would let students feel the problem before receiving the concept?
- What misconception does the theory correct?
- Which passage should students reread aloud or annotate slowly?

For a knowledge base:

- Create theory cards with `问题`, `核心命题`, `概念关系`, `适用范围`, `反例`, `引用`.
- Link the text to methods, measures, rival theories, and teaching cases.

### 着紧用力

Make reading finite and demanding:

- Choose a small unit for close work.
- Record unresolved questions immediately.
- Return to the same passage after a break.
- Prefer one precise difficulty over ten vague impressions.

### 居敬持志

Maintain attention as part of the method:

- Name the reading intention before starting.
- Avoid multitopic drift.
- If attention has scattered, pause and restate the paragraph's question.
- Treat confusion as a cue to narrow scope, not to generate a shallow summary.

## Deep Reading Prompts

Use these prompts as needed:

- `这一段到底在回答什么问题？`
- `作者为什么必须在这里引入这个概念？`
- `这段前人研究是被承接、批判、补足、整合，还是重定位？`
- `作者从前人那里保留了什么，又把问题推进到哪里？`
- `如果删掉这段前人研究，作者的新理论会少掉哪一块合法性或问题空间？`
- `作者在这里是在定义、澄清、比较，还是排除误读？`
- `这个概念和邻近概念差在哪里？差异只是词语不同，还是理论功能不同？`
- `作者为什么不用另一个更常见的词？这个术语选择增加了什么？`
- `这里的关键词在文中出现过几次，含义有没有变化？`
- `如果把这一句删掉，论证损失什么？`
- `作者反对的对象是谁，是明说还是暗含？`
- `这个理论解释了什么，又故意不解释什么？`
- `这里有哪些需要保留的歧义？`
- `我把它用于自己的研究时，哪些部分属于作者，哪些属于我的转化？`

## Failure Modes

- `贪多`: trying to process the whole book when one section is the real bottleneck.
- `先立说`: beginning with critique, ideology, or familiar frameworks before reconstructing the text.
- `浮泛`: producing polished summaries that cannot explain any paragraph's function.
- `泥碎`: losing the main pulse by over-fixating on isolated words.
- `空转`: saying the text is important without naming how it changes reading, research, or teaching.
